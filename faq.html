Common Questions

Straight Answers Before You Request Service

These are the questions customers ask most often when deciding how to move forward with a heavy-duty towing or transport call.

Helpful Reminder

  • Share the current location as precisely as possible.
  • Mention the type of truck, trailer, bus, RV, or equipment involved.
  • Explain whether the issue is a normal tow, roadside problem, or recovery situation.
What happens after I contact dispatch?

Dispatch reviews your location, vehicle type, condition, and destination so the right truck, operator, and equipment can be assigned.

Do you only handle emergency calls?

No. We assist with urgent roadside breakdowns and also support scheduled towing, relocation, and planned equipment transport.

What information should I have ready before I call?

The exact location, the kind of unit involved, the current issue, access conditions, and the destination all help dispatch respond more accurately.

Can I request service for a fleet, company yard, or job site?

Yes. Heavy-duty towing is often needed at terminals, fleet yards, industrial properties, and customer job sites, not just on the roadside.

Recovery Questions

Answers for Stuck, Damaged, or Difficult Access Jobs

Recovery work often needs more planning than a standard tow. These answers explain the type of details that help on the front end.

What should I mention for a recovery or winch-out?

Mention whether the vehicle is stuck in mud, off-road, in a ditch, on an incline, blocked in, or involved in an accident scene.

Can damaged commercial vehicles still be transported?

Yes, but dispatch needs accurate condition details so the transport plan can match the vehicle's limitations and scene requirements.

Do I need to know the exact recovery method before calling?

No. You only need to explain the situation clearly. Dispatch and the operator determine the safest approach based on the scene.

Recovery Details That Matter

Scene Conditions

Mud, soft ground, embankments, poor access, or blocked space can change the recovery setup.

Load and Trailer Status

A loaded trailer, detached unit, or special cargo situation may affect the response plan and equipment needed.

Safety Considerations

Let dispatch know if traffic exposure, accident damage, or unstable positioning makes the scene more sensitive.

Scheduling and Service Area

Questions About Timing, Service Types, and Coverage

Use these answers if you are planning a move, checking whether your vehicle type is a fit, or preparing details for a non-emergency request.

Can I schedule towing or transport in advance?

Yes. Scheduled service is useful for planned equipment moves, trailer relocations, fleet transfers, and non-urgent heavy towing work.

What kinds of units can you help move?

Common calls include semis, buses, box trucks, trailers, motorcoaches, RVs, work trucks, and many types of heavy equipment or attachments.

How is pricing usually determined?

Pricing depends on the unit involved, distance, access conditions, urgency, recovery complexity, and any special equipment needed for the job.

Do you only work on highways?

No. Heavy-duty calls often come from city routes, industrial properties, fleet yards, terminals, job sites, and customer facilities as well as interstates.

Good To Know

  • Planned transport requests usually move faster when pickup and drop-off details are confirmed early.
  • Large units, attached trailers, and damaged vehicles should always be described as clearly as possible.
  • Recovery conditions and restricted access can affect timing and dispatch preparation.
